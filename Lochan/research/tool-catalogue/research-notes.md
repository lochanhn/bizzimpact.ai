# AI tool catalogue expansion — findings

November 7, 2026 · selected official-page review

## Findings that change our product design

1. A product list needs dated evidence and review depth. The 100 additions cover seven business areas, while the assessment still covers three tasks. Tools outside those tasks are browsable, not force-matched.
2. Product pages do not prove plan entitlements. New product-only records cannot receive a confirmed fit score, even when the owner has no budget limit. Unknown free-plan status is a check, not proof that a tool is paid or free.
3. Some products have changed positioning or names: Rytr now presents client relationship/follow-up assistance; Writesonic emphasises AI-search visibility; Creatopy redirects to The Brief; Userlike redirects to Lime Connect; Coda advertises its Superhuman Docs transition. We preserve current names and context instead of copying an old directory.
4. Embedded AI deserves attention: QuickBooks, Xero, HubSpot, Pipedrive, Zoho CRM, Notion and other existing-workspace features may be worth checking before adding another system. This is a design inference, not evidence of their effectiveness for our users.
5. Trials, credit units and annual pricing are traps for simple ranking. Chatbase’s retrieved pricing toggle was ambiguous, so no numeric plan cost was guessed. Photoroom Pro excludes video despite the broader product offering video tools. Rosie’s Professional plan sends booking links; the higher Scale plan lists direct calendar booking.
6. Broad availability is not small-business suitability. Enterprise-oriented or developer platforms are labelled for additional setup/minimum-spend checks. Meeting recording, finance writes and generated media require their own human permissions and review. We did not inspect all legal/security policies.
